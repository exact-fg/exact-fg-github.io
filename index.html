Scope

Explainability and interpretability have received increasing attention in face verification and identification, with several recent studies proposing methods to reveal the evidence underlying biometric decisions. However, these approaches remain at an early stage and have not yet been extensively evaluated for robustness, reliability, and practical utility in real-world biometric scenarios. Moreover, existing attribution and explanation techniques are predominantly designed for static images and do not naturally extend to the temporal domain, where information is distributed across videos and sequences of frames.

These limitations highlight a critical need for explainability methods that can address the unique challenges of temporal and multimodal biometric data, while providing explanations that are not only visually or intuitively meaningful but also faithful to the underlying model’s decision process. Beyond developing explanation methods, it is equally important to establish rigorous criteria for explanation fidelity and to understand whether such explanations are genuinely useful to practitioners and end users in real-world settings.

The session will bring together emerging approaches that generate explanations for face- and gesture-based systems, methods for evaluating the fidelity, robustness, and reliability of these explanations, and studies investigating their usability and practical value in real-world applications.

The special session particularly welcomes novel explainability and interpretability methods for image- and video-based face and gesture analysis, including approaches that explicitly model temporal information, as well as work addressing broader biometric applications. By connecting methodological advances with rigorous evaluation and real-world considerations, the session aims to advance explainability from proof-of-concept demonstrations toward reliable and practically useful tools for biometric systems.

Topics covered

Topics include, but are not limited to, the following. Work addressing broader biometric applications is also welcome.

Explanation methods and interpretable architectures for biometrics

  • Attribution, saliency and feature-guided explanation for face recognition
  • Counterfactual and contrastive explanation of identity decisions
  • Concept-based, prototype-based and self-explainable architectures

Evaluation of explanations in biometric applications

  • Faithfulness, sufficiency, stability and sanity checks for attribution methods
  • Protocols, benchmarks and datasets for measuring explanation quality
  • Human-grounded evaluation, which asks whether an explanation changes the decision a person actually makes

Explanation of decisions on longitudinal and temporal data

  • Frame, segment and motion attribution for sequence-level decisions
  • Interpretability for gesture, sign-language and action recognition
  • Counterfactuals and interventions on behaviour over time

Human factors, forensics and contestability

  • Agreement and disagreement between automatic decision systems and forensic examiners (e.g. super-recognisers)
  • Evidential reporting and admissibility of automated comparison
  • Explanation of decisions across demographic groups to mitigate bias
